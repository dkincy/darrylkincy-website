ROUND1

THE STORY

How a broadcast kid became the man in the corner.

  1. The Voice

    Darryl trained as a broadcast major, learning how to hold a room with nothing but a message and a microphone. That voice became the tool of his life’s work: making young people actually hear that they matter.

  2. The Classrooms

    As a Community Relations Specialist at ITT Technical Institute in Sylmar, California, Darryl walked into classrooms and assemblies across Los Angeles — building bridges with school leaders and counselors, and showing students the value of what comes after graduation. Powerful, informative, entertaining — and impossible to ignore.

  3. Think Differently

    Through Think Differently LLC, Darryl mentored 16–24 year-olds fighting to earn their high school diplomas — working on the things a test can’t measure: character, discipline, and the belief that they were built for more.

  4. The KOrnerman

    In boxing, the cornerman is the one who patches you up between rounds, tells you the truth, and sends you back out swinging. That’s the role Darryl chose — in mentoring rooms and on the KOrnerman podcast, where he sits down with fathers, leaders, and fighters of every kind.

“A young man doesn’t quit because the fight is too hard.
He quits because nobody’s in his corner.”

— Darryl Kincy
ROUND2

THE FIGHT

The boys every teacher knows — and nobody reaches.

There’s a boy in every school: bright, restless, capable — and failing. His grades don’t match his mind. His behavior gets him sent out of the rooms he most needs to be in. His focus is a fight he loses daily.

Darryl specializes in that boy. He works with middle and high school boys who are underperforming academically and struggling to control their actions, behavior, and concentration — meeting them at their level, earning their respect, and training them like athletes: with standards, with reps, and with someone in their corner who refuses to give up on them.

01

BELIEVE

Before a young man changes his behavior, he has to change what he believes about himself. Darryl makes students believe in themselves and see the potential they have to succeed — that’s the first round of every engagement.

02

DISCIPLINE

Self-control is a trainable skill, not a personality trait. Boys learn to own their actions and their reactions — in the hallway, in the classroom, and at home.

03

FOCUS

Concentration is built like conditioning: one rep at a time. Students build routines and habits that turn scattered energy into academic progress teachers can see.

For schools, districts, and youth programs, the result is the outcome you actually need: fewer removals from class, restored teacher relationships, and boys re-engaged in their own education.
